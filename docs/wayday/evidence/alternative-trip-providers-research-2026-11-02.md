# Alternative providers for Your trip — research, 2026-11-02

Status: research and recommendation only. The owner requested a comparison before implementation. No provider adoption, account, purchase, deployment, credential change or runtime change is authorized by this document. Prices and public documentation were checked on 2026-11-02; account entitlements, Philippine field quality and native compatibility remain unverified.

## Recommendation

Keep Google as the default and add one coherent OpenStreetMap-based fallback: the existing MapLibre renderer, an independently hosted basemap, and Geoapify Routing/Route Matrix. Preserve Wayday's local trip records, scheduler and completion controls. Qualify a native MapLibre Navigation integration for embedded guidance before advertising it as equivalent to the current Your trip experience. A map with a route line and written steps is useful, but it is not a replacement for a navigation engine.

This is the best initial fit for the existing code and zero-spending preference. It avoids replacing the working Google integration and reuses a provider already integrated for search. It does not establish that Geoapify is more available or more accurate than Google. Independence from one provider and local recovery improve resilience; neither free hosting nor a second API guarantees uninterrupted navigation.

If the priority becomes a supported Flutter SDK with offline route calculation and guidance, evaluate HERE Navigate commercially. It is a stronger packaged alternative, subject to approved pricing and Philippine qualification, rather than a zero-cost drop-in.

Geoapify's route endpoint returns GeoJSON/JSON/XML, not a verified drop-in MapLibre Navigation route object. A converter must preserve maneuvers, legs and waypoint boundaries; written instructions alone do not prove timed voice guidance works. The matrix supports up to 1,000 cells and null unreachable pairs. A null pair must remain unavailable. LocationIQ's optional straight-line `fallback_speed` must not be enabled as a substitute for road times. [Geoapify matrix](https://apidocs.geoapify.com/docs/route-matrix/), [LocationIQ matrix](https://docs.locationiq.com/docs/matrix-api).

No comparable uptime or Philippine route-quality benchmark was established by this research. Geoapify publishes a default 99.5% monthly availability SLA for paid plans; the free tier must not be presented as SLA-backed. Two independently hosted OSM services can reduce exposure to a vendor outage, but may share missing road/access data and other infrastructure dependencies. A provider switch cannot repair every bad destination, missing road or device/network failure. [Geoapify availability terms](https://www.geoapify.com/pricing/).

For scale, a short route with six total waypoints costs five basic Geoapify routing credits. A separately requested 6×6 matrix costs 36 credits; 100 map tiles cost 25. These are calculated examples, excluding discovery, enhanced fields, long-route surcharges and reroutes. An accepted-order trip generally needs its ordered route, not another all-pairs matrix. Geoapify's search and routing consume the same account allocation. [Credit schedule](https://www.geoapify.com/pricing-details/).

## Basemaps and offline coverage

Use the existing OpenFreeMap-compatible MapLibre path as the initial independent online basemap, with visible OpenStreetMap/provider attribution. OpenFreeMap advertises no map-view/request charges, but its terms offer no availability warranty and allow discontinuation. An outage fallback should therefore also support a complete downloaded regional package, not depend exclusively on this public host. [Service](https://openfreemap.org/), [terms](https://openfreemap.org/tos/).

OpenFreeMap currently permits limited offline downloads: at most 3,000 tiles or 50 MB per download, four parallel requests, and an identifying User-Agent; larger usage needs agreement. Do not split a larger download merely to evade those limits. The existing Pasig/Ortigas package is a bounded map preview, not Philippine-wide offline navigation. For packaged coverage, styles, fonts and sprites must also be local. Standard `tile.openstreetmap.org` prohibits offline prefetch and has no SLA; do not use that endpoint as Wayday's download backend. [OpenFreeMap terms](https://openfreemap.org/tos/), [OSMF tile policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/).

There are three distinct offline promises: open local trip details; follow a legally retained route with downloaded maps/instructions; calculate a new route offline. The last requires an on-device routing graph/engine or a licensed offline SDK. PMTiles/vector map imagery does not supply that graph. Geoapify states that results may be cached/stored under its terms, which makes its own route snapshots a candidate; retain retrieval/version information and review attribution/storage conditions before defining that snapshot. Google retention restrictions remain separate. [Geoapify storage FAQ](https://www.geoapify.com/).

## Navigation engine choice

Investigate **MapLibre Navigation Android/KMP first**, behind a native Kotlin bridge and provider-neutral Dart events. Its release page lists 5.0.1, while README snippets still mention 5.0.0-pre13; pin and inspect a released artifact rather than copying those snippets. The 5.0.1 source uses MapLibre Native 13.6.1, close to Wayday's strict 13.7.1 pin, but this is not evidence of binary compatibility. Prove the combined dependency graph, API-24 support, release/R8 behavior and renderer lifecycle before adoption. [Releases](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-navigation-android/releases), [tagged version catalog](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-navigation-android/blob/5.0.1/gradle/libs.versions.toml).

**Ferrostar** is a credible second navigation engine. Its official documentation describes Flutter integration through native classes/views and custom route providers; it is not a ready-made supported Flutter package. It remains labelled beta without API stability guarantees. Its documented Android floor is API 25, whereas Wayday currently supports API 24. Do not raise Wayday's minimum silently. It may be attractive for a later dedicated bridge, especially with Valhalla-compatible output. It supplies navigation logic, not its own basemap, routing graph or free hosted service. [Overview](https://stadiamaps.github.io/ferrostar/), [platform targets](https://stadiamaps.github.io/ferrostar/platform-support-targets.html), [route adapters](https://stadiamaps.github.io/ferrostar/route-providers.html).

Avoid hand-building driving guidance from just nearest-polyline distance and a TTS package. GPS uncertainty, wrong carriageways, maneuver progression, missed turns and prompt cancellation require a qualified engine. Evaluate native guidance under Wayday's existing foreground lifecycle; an SDK tutorial's foreground/background-service setup does not authorize new background tracking.

## Google content boundaries

Google Routes results must not be displayed on a non-Google map. Navigation SDK content and standard Places API content also have non-Google-map restrictions. Consequently, moving the existing Google polyline, ETA panel and enriched business rows onto MapLibre is not the proposed implementation. Recompute alternative routes and use independently obtained eligible place information. [Routes policy](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/policies), [Places SDK policy](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/android-sdk/policies).

There is an important exception: current service-specific terms permit Places UI Kit with a non-Google map. Its separate attribution, export/derived-content and caching restrictions still apply. That exception does not cover arbitrary custom Places SDK recommendation metadata or Google Routes. It must not be interpreted as unconditional permission to export every UI Kit coordinate to a different routing service. Preserve product-level provenance and confirm permitted use before enabling any cross-provider transfer. [Service-specific terms, sections 12, 14, 15 and 19](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms).

For a dependable fallback, prefer independently confirmed MapLibre pins, device GPS fixes and alternative-provider references. Google-only stops can require confirmation using the existing Other sources/map-pin flow. Do not auto-select a similarly named branch, relabel a Google coordinate as user-owned, extend an expired lease, copy Google addresses/hours into a permanent fallback record, or treat a thirty-day coordinate cache permission as permission to retain an entire route. Existing Google references remain available for returning to the Google path.

## Proposed experience and implementation boundaries

Offer a simple Your trip navigation-source choice: **Automatic**, **Google**, **OpenStreetMap**. Automatic should first preserve access to the local checklist and offer the qualified alternative after a bounded service failure; do not poll both providers continuously or change a working active navigator without a visible transition. Users should be able to choose the alternative before Google SDK initialization. Keep provider names and reduced functionality explicit.

Preserve the current one-errand panel, saved completion before advancing, shared-stop task identities, later return visits, Whole trip, Recenter and Finish behavior. A provider change keeps the same tasks, selected branches and accepted order. It invalidates old road geometry/times and computes the remaining route using the new provider. Recheck appointment/hour feasibility with eligible fresh data; unknown hours stay unknown. Do not silently reorder stops, drop required work or regenerate Gemini.

Suggested recovery copy is specific: “Google navigation is unavailable. Your trip is saved. Use OpenStreetMap navigation” when that capability is qualified; otherwise say “View trip with OpenStreetMap” and disclose that turn-by-turn guidance is unavailable. Offer independently confirmed location repair only for affected stops. Keep local completion usable even if both providers fail.

Before an implementation commitment, qualify a small vertical proof containing the MapLibre renderer, alternative route response, native navigation driver and saved completion transition. The implementation would need:

1. Provider-neutral coordinate, route, maneuver, progress, failure and capability types, replacing Google types at map/navigation boundaries.
2. Per-location provenance/retention eligibility and separate provider references without overwriting Google choices.
3. A bounded alternative route adapter; matrices only for authorized planning/revalidation work. Preserve asymmetric road pairs, context identity, freshness and in-flight coalescing.
4. A read-only local trip view that remains independent of map/service startup. Add legally retainable alternative route/map snapshots separately if offline following is approved.
5. A native navigation driver with explicit waypoint advancement and the existing lifecycle protections. Adapt route data rather than assuming provider formats are interchangeable.
6. Structured endpoint-specific failures and a circuit breaker: distinguish maps, routing, Places, GPS/permission, authorization and quota failures. Do not repeatedly retry denied credentials, ping-pong providers or represent a failed route as an empty map.
7. Server-backed private routing keys for public builds, bounded allowlisted requests and distinct provider-credit accounting. Changes to frozen released gateway contracts must be additive/versioned. Cloud/secret-reference/authentication changes need separate review before deployment.

The initial scope should recover an existing accepted trip. Creating a new fully automatic plan during a Google Places outage additionally requires non-Google recommendation discovery/details/hours qualification. Existing Other sources/manual confirmation can help, but the search tab alone does not make the current automatic recommendation pipeline provider-independent.

## Outage coverage and limits

| Failure | Expected recovery target |
| --- | --- |
| Google map/routing service unavailable, internet works | Eligible independent places + MapLibre + alternative routing/guidance after qualification; keep local progress throughout. |
| Google Places unavailable | Previously eligible independent references may work. Google-only/expired locations require alternative confirmation; do not substitute a guessed business. |
| Alternative routing quota exhausted | Preserve trip/checklist and any permitted retained route; offer explicit retry/provider choice, without automatic paid expansion. |
| Both online map services unavailable | A complete downloaded package can display its covered area; otherwise keep the readable stop list. |
| Internet lost after a fallback route was prepared | Retained permitted route/maps/instructions may support following that route. Fresh rerouting remains unavailable without a local routing engine. |
| Trip reopened offline without a prepared alternative route or usable coordinates | Open local tasks/progress immediately; explain unavailable map/guidance honestly. |
| Broader Google Cloud/Firebase outage | Existing App Check/Cloud Run transport is still a shared dependency; changing the upstream router does not solve this. Local recovery must bypass network dependency, not authentication enforcement. Complete online cloud independence requires a separately reviewed hosting/authentication design. |
| Native SDK crash | A process death cannot be caught and converted into an in-process map switch. Retain trip state, allow source selection before restart initialization, and validate release artifacts/device behavior. |
| GPS unavailable | Changing map vendors does not restore GPS; show uncertainty and retain manual checklist access. |

## Qualification before publication

Use mocked provider failures and the guarded disposable emulator first. Cover switching at startup/mid-trip, timeouts versus still-running requests, 401/403/429/5xx, empty/unreachable routes, expired references, restart without internet, incomplete downloads, GPS loss/recovery, missed turns, long Filipino street names, speech cancellation, repeated opening/closing, shared stops, later return visits and failed completion writes. Confirm that Google content cannot enter the alternative map and no provider switch regenerates Gemini or resets accounting.

Build the minified signed release and inspect dependency/R8 behavior before phone qualification. Independently test Philippine driving, walking, bicycle and motorcycle routes, road access, ferries, one-way streets and gated entrances. Compare measured road/travel behavior rather than treating global coverage or provider marketing as local accuracy evidence. Truck profiles and dimension parameters do not establish clearance-aware Philippine routing: retain the existing explicitly confirmed compatible light-pickup/ordinary-driving boundary until separately qualified.

Record build, mocked/emulator, provider-live and unplugged physical-device results separately. No such new runtime qualification was performed here. No spending, extra funds, billing activation, personal-phone harness or installation/reset is part of this research.
